AI Readiness in Dental Schools: department and course questionnaire

Thank you for helping us build the first international, course-level map of artificial intelligence in dental education.

Purpose of the form

What this questionnaire is for

Most studies on AI in dental education ask students and teachers what they think about AI. Very few measure what schools actually provide. This questionnaire documents, for one named department or course, the resources, people, teaching and policies that support AI today.

Answers from Canada, the United States, Brazil, China and India are combined into a comparable maturity index for each department, school and country. Schools can then see where they stand and what to prioritize.

How it works

Four simple steps

  1. Short call with our team

    We confirm your department, course and role, and record your consent.

  2. You receive a personal code

    A 6-character code in the format XXX-XXX, linked only to your department or course.

  3. Answer at your own pace

    About 30–40 minutes. Answers save at each section, so you can stop and return with the same code.

  4. Submit and receive your benchmark

    After submission your answers are locked. Participating schools receive a confidential comparison report.

Answer every item only for the department or course named in your invitation. Choose "Don't know" rather than guessing. Approximate numbers are fine.

End of the first approach

Results we will deliver

At the end of the first data-collection round, ICARB-D will produce:

1. A course-level AI maturity map

A 0–100 readiness index for every participating department, consolidated by school (weighted by number of courses) and by country, with domain-by-domain heat maps.

2. Benchmark of AI teaching hours

Hours per year of AI teaching by topic, including programming and formal model development, at undergraduate, graduate and residency level.

3. Inventory of AI tools and infrastructure

Which AI tools students actually use, for which clinical tasks, and with what regulatory approval, plus computing, datasets and technical support.

4. Faculty capacity and training gaps

The share of faculty able to teach AI, AI research activity, training received and the barriers that slow progress.

5. Governance and generative-AI policy profile

Status of AI plans, ethics and bias teaching, generative-AI rules and an AI tool-governance index adapted from PAHO/WHO AI-GUARD.

6. Recommendations and publications

Country-specific priorities, a peer-reviewed article, an open codebook and a confidential benchmark report for each participating school.

Confidentiality

Results are published only in aggregate. No school, department or person is identified without permission. Your name and email are stored separately from your answers and are used only to contact you about this study.

Who should answer

The department head, course director, program director or a faculty member designated by them. Each named course has its own code. If your department runs several AI-related courses, you may receive several codes.
