Purpose of the form

What this questionnaire is for

Main goal: build a benchmark — one comparable number from 0 to 100 that measures how mature each US dental school is in health informatics, with a score for each of its areas. The same questions also work as a checklist of what each course or department is missing to reach the level of maturity it wants, a guide for decision makers to improve health informatics.

As a practical conclusion, the benchmark helps answer a funding question: if a grant for health informatics in dentistry were available, which university would have the best chance of using it well? Comparing schools, and comparing US schools with universities abroad that answer the same survey, are secondary uses.

Health informatics is the umbrella. Its areas are health information systems (records, imaging, coding, teledentistry), artificial intelligence, robotics and digital dentistry, bioinformatics, and software development for health. All rest on the same foundations: infrastructure, data and privacy, people, governance, and implementation.

Who answers: a dental school is organised as university → departments → courses. Each course director answers one form for their course; department scores are built from their courses, and the university benchmark from its departments.

The idea comes from the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), which created a questionnaire to measure how ready public health institutions are to use AI. This survey adapts its dimensions and 1–4 maturity scale to dental schools. It is repeated every year, and after answering, the same items work as a maturity checklist.

How it works

Four simple steps

  1. Short call with our team

    We confirm your university, department and course, and record your consent.

  2. You receive a personal code

    A 6-character code in the format XXX-XXX, linked only to your course.

  3. Answer at your own pace

    About 45–60 minutes. Answers save at each section, so you can stop and return with the same code.

  4. Submit and receive your benchmark

    After submission your answers are locked. Participating schools receive a confidential comparison report.

Answer every item for the course named in your invitation. Choose "Don't know" rather than guessing. Approximate numbers are fine.

End of the first approach

Results we will deliver

At the end of the first annual wave, ICARB-D will produce:

1. A benchmark per university

One 0–100 number of health informatics maturity for every participating dental school, built from its courses and departments, with a score for each area. It shows which schools are best placed to use new funding well, and later waves add year-to-year trends.

2. Benchmark of teaching hours

Hours per year by topic for AI (including programming and formal model development), health informatics, robotics and bioinformatics, at undergraduate, graduate and residency level.

3. Inventory of physical resources

AI tools students actually use and their regulatory status; counts of robots, simulators, scanners, CBCT, milling units and 3D printers; laboratories, biobanks, computing and datasets.

4. Human resources and training gaps

Faculty able to teach each domain, researchers, technical staff, training received and the barriers that slow progress.

5. Governance and generative-AI policy profile

Status of AI plans, ethics and bias teaching, generative-AI rules and an AI tool-governance index adapted from PAHO/WHO AI-GUARD.

6. A plan for the open dental AI model

Which departments can contribute data, annotation and validation, under which conditions; plus national priorities, a peer-reviewed article, an open codebook and a confidential benchmark for each school.

Confidentiality

Results are published only in aggregate. No school, department or person is identified without permission. Your name and email are stored separately from your answers and are used only to contact you about this study.

Who should answer

The director (head) of each participating course. Each course has its own form and code, so a department with several relevant courses sends several forms. A new code is issued each year.
