Purpose of the form

What this questionnaire is for

The survey gives a comprehensive overview of how mature each department or course is in artificial intelligence, health informatics, robotics and digital dentistry, and bioinformatics.

For each domain it pictures the physical resources (computers, AI tools, robotic and digital equipment, laboratories, data) and the human resources (faculty able to teach, researchers, technical staff, partners). It also measures teaching hours by topic, governance, sustainability, and each department's capacity to contribute to the consortium's free, open dental AI model.

The survey is repeated every year. Each wave maps the state of the art across universities and courses in Canada, the United States, Brazil, China and India, and comparing waves shows how each department develops.

How it works

Four simple steps

  1. Short call with our team

    We confirm your department, course and role, and record your consent.

  2. You receive a personal code

    A 6-character code in the format XXX-XXX, linked only to your department or course.

  3. Answer at your own pace

    About 45–60 minutes. Answers save at each section, so you can stop and return with the same code.

  4. Submit and receive your benchmark

    After submission your answers are locked. Participating schools receive a confidential comparison report.

Answer every item only for the department or course named in your invitation. Choose "Don't know" rather than guessing. Approximate numbers are fine.

End of the first approach

Results we will deliver

At the end of the first annual wave, ICARB-D will produce:

1. A course-level digital maturity map

A 0–100 index for AI, health informatics, robotics and bioinformatics for every participating department, consolidated by school (weighted by number of courses) and by country, with heat maps. Later waves add year-to-year trends.

2. Benchmark of teaching hours

Hours per year by topic for AI (including programming and formal model development), health informatics, robotics and bioinformatics, at undergraduate, graduate and residency level.

3. Inventory of physical resources

AI tools students actually use and their regulatory status; counts of robots, simulators, scanners, CBCT, milling units and 3D printers; laboratories, biobanks, computing and datasets.

4. Human resources and training gaps

Faculty able to teach each domain, researchers, technical staff, training received and the barriers that slow progress.

5. Governance and generative-AI policy profile

Status of AI plans, ethics and bias teaching, generative-AI rules and an AI tool-governance index adapted from PAHO/WHO AI-GUARD.

6. A plan for the open dental AI model

Which departments can contribute data, annotation and validation, under which conditions; plus country priorities, a peer-reviewed article, an open codebook and a confidential benchmark for each school.

Confidentiality

Results are published only in aggregate. No school, department or person is identified without permission. Your name and email are stored separately from your answers and are used only to contact you about this study.

Who should answer

The department head, course director, program director or a faculty member designated by them. Each named course has its own code. If your department runs several relevant courses, you may receive several codes. A new code is issued each year.
