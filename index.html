About the consortium

Why a consortium?

No single school has enough data, equipment or expertise to build trustworthy AI for dentistry alone. By joining forces, universities and dentists can pool de-identified data, share annotation and validation work, and develop a free dental AI model that can be used across every field of dentistry, serving patients and students everywhere, not only where resources are abundant.

Artificial intelligence, robotics and bioinformatics are entering dental practice faster than dental schools can adapt. AI already reads radiographs, robots assist in implant surgery, and multi-omics research is reshaping how we understand oral disease. Yet there is little shared evidence on how schools teach these fields, what infrastructure they have, and who is prepared to lead the change.

ICARB-D was created to close that gap through international collaboration. Common instruments, open methods and comparable data let schools learn from each other and plan with evidence.

Mission

What we do

  1. Connect

    Be the meeting point for universities and dentists running AI, robotics and bioinformatics projects in dentistry.

  2. Measure

    Map every year the maturity of dental schools in AI, health informatics, robotics and bioinformatics, department by department.

  3. Build

    Develop together a free dental AI model for use across every field of dentistry, along with shared datasets, curricula and tools.

  4. Share

    Publish open methods, codebooks and peer-reviewed results, and give each participating school a confidential benchmark.

Three domains

One consortium, three converging fields

Each domain is studied across education, research and clinical care.

Artificial intelligence

From diagnostic imaging to large language models in teaching.

  • AI-assisted radiograph and CBCT analysis
  • Clinical decision support and treatment planning
  • Generative AI literacy, ethics and bias

Robotics

Precision, simulation and automation in dental care and training.

  • Robot-assisted implant and endodontic procedures
  • Haptic simulators with automated feedback
  • Digital workflows, CAD/CAM and 3D printing

Bioinformatics

Turning biological data into oral-health knowledge.

  • Oral microbiome and salivary omics
  • Genomics of oral disease and craniofacial traits
  • Data standards, sharing and reproducible pipelines

Annual survey · first wave 2026

Digital maturity of dental schools

Every year, ICARB-D maps how mature each dental school department and course is in AI, health informatics, robotics and bioinformatics: its physical resources, its people, its teaching hours and its governance. Department heads and course directors answer a structured questionnaire with a personal access code.
